# How Difficulty 11 is implemented

<!-- diataxis: explanation -->

This page is for people working on HellForge itself. It explains how `add_difficulty` adds a level after Super
Helldive: which game tables move or grow, what each part of the level changes, and what it checks first. The mod's side
is in [modding/content.md](modding/content.md), and the rest of the mod machinery in [internals.md](internals.md).

`add_difficulty` adds level 11 after Super Helldive. It is a copy of level 10's settings (enemy mix, packages, mission
rules) with its own title, description, unlock item and enemy budgets. The budgets are `encounter_points`,
`guardforce_points` and `spawner_cooldown`, and level 10 has 600, 600 and 1.5 s. One mod can add it.

## The difficulty table

The game keeps its 10 difficulties in a static table of 0x330-byte rows, in 19 identical copies. HellForge moves it
once `game.dll` has filled it. One table with 11 rows replaces every copy for the 57 instructions that read them
(`native/mod-loader/difficulty_sites.h`). That file is generated by `artifacts/difficulty11/difficulty_sites.py`, which
is not committed, and the research is in `FINDINGS.md` there.

The difficulty API's limits of 10 become 11:

- the highest level;
- row and level lookups by unlock item;
- the unlocked mask and highest unlocked level.

Every pinned instruction, limit and copy is checked first. Anything else refuses the change, and the game keeps its 10
levels.

Level 11 is published in the lobby like any other level, so friends need the same mod (the modlist gate checks it).

`examples/mods/hell-forge` adds Hell Forge:

- 1500 encounter and guardforce points, and a 1 s spawner cooldown;
- 8 optional and 2 secondary objectives;
- three times the outposts;
- a 72-minute countdown;
- twice the map radius;
- patrols twice as often and up to 1.5 times as many;
- the M-102 Gunner FRV granted in every mission;
- every flag below;
- its own icon.

## Choosing it

On the war table, level 11 is the carousel's eleventh place, after Super Helldive. The bar's object holds exactly 10
widgets, one per level, and another member follows them. So level 11 gets an eleventh widget of its own.

The bar's build, update, destroy and choice loops run to 11. The five instructions that turn an index into a widget
address go through stubs that give index 10 the bar's own extra widget (a pool of 32, one per bar). The band sprite
behind each icon comes from a 10-entry array read by level. That array moves too (level 11 takes level 10's).

## Icon and name

`HfDifficulty.icon` names an icon the mod's data spec builds (`"icon"` with `"prepare": "image"`, archive
`007e093ca718ca1a`). The bar's own image setter takes atlas sprites only, so for level 11 its icon call goes through a
stub. The stub sets Super Helldive's sprite first, which gives the image element its resource. Then it sets the mod's
texture, with the whole texture as its UV rect.

Other places that draw a level's icon take atlas sprites only, such as the war table's mission markers. So level 11's
row keeps Super Helldive's sprite for them. The bar draws the icon in 200 x 138, so make the art fill the square
texture. The title is shown in capitals, as the game's names are.

## Enemies

Level 11 has its own enemy tech level, 11. Each roster row (0x80) holds a spawn weight per tech level at +0x30, with 8
unused bytes before them. So the weights move 4 bytes down, and level 11's weight (level 10's to begin with) goes after
them. The six readers' displacements follow.

That is every row of all six rosters. The sixth roster (0x21CB470, one row) is read by the same readers. Left in place,
it read level 1 as 0 and had no composition at 11.

Compositions allowing up to tech 10 allow 11 (319 rows), and the director's clamp of the tech level becomes 11. The
level gets its own budgets. A single enemy group still holds at most 32 enemies, since more crashes the game. So bigger
budgets mean more groups more often.

## Unlocking

Each level's row names an inventory item that unlocks it. Level 11's item is a copy of Super Helldive's in the backend's
catalogue (`registered-items.json`, `src/HellForge.LocalBackend/RegisteredItems.cs`). A player who holds Super
Helldive's item holds it too. Nothing is saved to the profile.

## The live cap

The game client configuration carries Arrowhead's cap on the level (online override 48726272, "10"). The game uses the
lower of it and its own maximum. With a difficulty registered, the backend serves the cap as 11.

## Rewards

Level 11 pays half as much XP and medals again as level 10: 2250 XP and 15 medals for a won mission (level 10: 1500 and
10), and 2200 requisition (`src/HellForge.LocalBackend/MissionService.cs`).

## Patrols

`patrol_frequency` divides the row's per-faction patrol interval (+0x2A0..). Level 10's is 65 / 78 / 75 s for
Terminids / Automatons / Illuminate. `patrol_cap` scales the most living patrol enemies (+0x2AC..), 41 / 39 / 48 at
level 10. The HiveMind scales that further by mission progress and players. The director reads both from the row every
frame.

## Flags

`flags` takes these. The first three have their own sections below.

- `HF_DIFFICULTY_SECOND_PRIMARY` makes every mission at the level two missions in one.
- `HF_DIFFICULTY_CITY_STRONGHOLDS` gives the level's city maps the strongholds its outpost counts ask for.
- `HF_DIFFICULTY_CITY_ICBM` makes every city operation at the level start with a Launch ICBM mission.
- `HF_DIFFICULTY_NO_ERADICATE` keeps Eradicate missions (their game mode's delta) at level 10 and below. Their kill
  objectives remain second main objectives.
- `HF_DIFFICULTY_NO_RAPID_ACQUISITION` and `HF_DIFFICULTY_NO_BLITZ` keep Rapid Acquisition and Blitz missions (mission
  kind 6) from being offered at the level.
- `HF_DIFFICULTY_RADAR` gives every mission at the level the radar station, marked on the map from the start. The radar
  station is an optional objective that reveals the map.

`HF_DIFFICULTY_HEAVY_ARTILLERY` is under [Artillery](#artillery). The mission-end telemetry keeps 16 objectives (capped).

`mission_stratagem` names a stratagem type every mission at the level grants every player. It goes in the mission group
with Reinforce and Resupply: no loadout slot, and the type's own uses and cooldown. The group grants it whether the
player owns it or not, so the loader refuses a paid type (`GrantRefused` in `difficulty_missions.c`):

- It reads the type's StratagemInfo row (+4, the stable id). A mod's type is checked by its own stable id and its base's.
- The paid stable ids come from `data/paid-items.json` through `native/mod-loader/paid_stratagems.h`, which
  `sdk-headers` generates and its check keeps current.
- The level's install waits until the game has loaded that row. A refused type is logged, and the level grants none.

The check runs in each player's loader. How a joiner's game handles a mission group the host sends (RPC 0xC953DB6D)
is not traced, so a changed host's paid stratagem may still reach it.

The header's descriptions of these
flags and fields are in `native/mod-sdk/hellforge_mod.h` (`HfDifficulty`).

### Second primary

`HF_DIFFICULTY_SECOND_PRIMARY` makes every mission at the level two missions in one. At the end of objective selection
(0x1756730, every caller: war table, mission, preview), the loader picks a partner mission. It must be:

- of the same faction;
- a standard mission an operation lists with this one;
- placed on this map's layout;
- with main objectives this mission does not have.

The pick is deterministic from the mission's seed, row and faction, so it is the same on every HellForge client. The
loader runs the game's own selection for it and appends its main objectives and their steps (class 0 and 1). The steps
are registered in the base missions' rows as class 1 with weight 0, so the game gates every main objective on all
steps of both missions. With weight 0 they are never drawn, so levels 1 to 10 are unchanged.

### City strongholds

`HF_DIFFICULTY_CITY_STRONGHOLDS` gives the level's city maps the strongholds (Automaton fortresses, Terminid mega nests)
its outpost counts ask for. The outposts set's two stronghold groups get copies of their items up to 100 m across,
appended at level init. The copies have class list {3} (the city layout's only class) and minimum difficulty 11, so only
cities at the level take them. Open maps keep theirs.

### City ICBM

`HF_DIFFICULTY_CITY_ICBM` makes every city operation at the level start with a Launch ICBM mission. Three of the game's
unused mission rows, one per faction, become city rows for levels 11..11. Each is the faction's city row with:

- the ICBM row's name;
- gen_icbm x 3 and the codes, power and refuelling steps;
- the city's side objectives.

They are listed first in the city operations, with a weight that always wins the operation's first (kind 1) mission.
The ICBM objectives' stamps get city copies the same way as the strongholds. These rows are not mixed with partner
missions.

## Objectives, outposts and map

### Objectives and outposts

Objective definitions allowing up to level 10 allow 11 (148 of them). Without this a level-11 mission had no objectives
at all.

`optional_objectives` and `major_objectives` set the row's counts (+0x28, +0x2C), and a mission's own list limits them.
Together they are at most 18. With more objectives, the map generator's objective list moves from 16 to 30 places, and
its mission stamp list from 128 to 1024. Both move to the end of the enlarged generation block. Up to 12 of the 30 are
the mission's own, its live event's and a partner mission's.

`outpost_scale` sets the row's outposts per size and faction (+0x34..).

### Countdown

`mission_minutes` sets every mission's countdown. It is an entity delta of its own, registered in the game's entity
delta table (HiveMind +0x74 = the level's seconds). It is added to a level-11 mission's delta list at launch
(0xAD0E4E), after the mission's own deltas, as High Value Asset missions get 1200 s. The shared definition is never
written, so other levels keep their countdown. The countdown's network definition (207, 0..2500 s, clamped by the host
every frame) is widened to fit.

### Map size

`map_scale` sets the row's playable radius factors (+0xF0.., up to 2). The map generator's route cells are a Poisson disc
of 40 m spacing within 680 m of the world's centre. They fill fixed arrays without checking them, so a larger playable
area alone overflows them. Above 1, each mission at the level gets:

- a disk that covers its border, at most 1022 m (the world is 2048 m);
- a spacing that keeps level 10's number of cells, in quarter metres.

Map type 1 lays them as a grid, and an inexact grid splits its corners into extra vertices. At 2 x this gives 1022 m and
65.75 m. Level init writes the level's radii (game.dll 0xF71CD8, after the map type is known). The two spacing reads
(0xF3614D, 0xF3732C) take the level's.

### Voronoi passes

GenerateVoronoi (0xF3F8E0) lays a 16 m grid over the same square, keeps the points in the disk and builds their Voronoi
diagram (jc_voronoi). Level 10 nearly fills its unchecked arrays: 5672 of 5680 sites, 5839 of 5849 vertices. So its
spacing grows with the disk too (0xF3FB04), rounded up to a quarter metre so the grid points stay exact in float.

An inexact spacing makes near-degenerate circles and extra vertices, and overruns the vertex array. The pass then
retries forever (the war table's "Receiving Geodetic Data"). At 2 x the spacing is 24.5 m.

GenerateRouteCellVoronoi (op 3, 0xF93800) fills each route cell with points at its type's size / 2 or / 4. Level 10
makes 11954 areas of 13224, in proportion to the generated area. So that spacing grows with the disk too (0xF9420F,
x 1.04, in quarter metres).

On map type 1, FinalizeVoronoi (0xF68050) rebuilds the route cells from points over the whole square at each cell
type's size. It is scaled the same way (0xF68273). Otherwise 2.25 x the points overrun the 1024 route cells.

### Terrain height

GenerateSubregionHeights (op 4, 0xF64E30) gives each area its cell type's curve at `t = clamp(d / 64 m)`. Here `d` is
the distance to the nearest route point (the 1/64 at 0xF651FA). With the cells farther apart, far more ground lies at
`t = 1`: flat terraces, and lake curves (4-13 m below 0) at their deepest under objectives.

An objective in a low cell takes a corner height of exactly 0 (0xF3B957). So its pad followed the area layer under the
region's water (-0.25 to -4.5 m), and an ICBM silo's lower platform flooded. Above 1:

- the ramp is 64 m x spacing / 40 (difficulty_terrain.c);
- the two stores of the area height (0xF655A9, 0xF656F9) keep it at or above 0 within an objective's stamp
  radius + 16 m.

Objectives are found among the important locations (G+0x8996B0, 0x108 each, count G+0x11A715C). The radius is the
location item's +0xD8.

### Artillery

The SEAF Artillery fires the projectile of the shell at the head of its queue. Its six shells' rows (71, 339, 99, 145,
10, 92) fly alike: 300 m/s, gravity x 10, drag 0.5. That reaches about 710 m, and a 2 x map is up to 1550 m across. The
aim (0x617E50, solver 0x13B1BD0) and the landing prediction (0x13B18B0) read speed and gravity from the row the shell
flies with.

Above 1, a watch poll (difficulty_artillery.c) sets each row in a mission at the level (the world's level generator,
+0xF9): speed x sqrt(map_scale) and drag x 1 / map_scale. That keeps k v^2 / g, so the
arc scales by map_scale and the aim follows it. In a mission at another level the rows are put back where they still
hold what was written.

`HF_DIFFICULTY_HEAVY_ARTILLERY` changes the side objective's shells. Its stamps place 12 to 28 spawn points (unit
C5A8CB32B972E580). Each rolls one shell (0x9F3250) from the unit's script data table 0x3268FC21 of {shell kind
(ObjectiveShell +0x10), weight}. The weights are Explosive 20, Napalm, Smoke and Static Field 10, High-Yield 5, Mini
Nuke 1.

The roll skips weights of 0 or less and picks by weight with the objective's seeded random numbers. A stub at the
weight's load (0x9F342F) gives a level-11 mission Explosive 10, High-Yield 5 and Mini Nuke 5, and every other kind 0.

### Stamp index and locations

The generator's stamp index (0xC04B80, for overlap tests of stamps, locations and roads) has 10757 slots. Level 10 uses
4892, and its 17 inserters don't check for a full index. Above 1 it gets 16383 (the same 14 index bits).

The level's locations (objectives, points of interest, outposts, ...) are two arrays of 2740 in the generation block:
G+0x758E20, 0xE0 each, and G+0x7EEBA0, 0x40 each. Level 10 makes about 824. 2 x maps with Hell Forge's objectives and
outposts made 3900 to 5500.

Above 1 the block grows (0x11A7268 -> 0x13E7280 bytes) and both arrays move to its end with 8192 places. That takes 223
instructions, 236 builder capacities and the block's size at 6 places (`location_sites.h`). The level's location types
(G+0x756C20, 96 x 0x10) move after them with 512 places (99 instructions, 92 capacities). Level 10 uses about 44
location types, and 2 x maps 73..81.

The minimap's arrays follow with 4 x their places:

- proxies, G+0x8198A0, 4096 x 0x30;
- scatter, G+0x8498A0, 2048 x 0x58;
- proxy units, G+0x118BEFC and G+0x118FEFC, 4096 and 16384 x 4.

They grow because a 2 x map's proxies ran over the scatter into the terrain merge slots (G+0x8758A8). The block is then
0x15A9280 bytes.

### Capacity a mod asks for

`generation_capacity` lets any mod ask for more places in five of the moved arrays: locations, minimap, mission stamps,
places and place set. The layout is therefore worked out, not fixed (`GenerationLayoutOf` in `difficulty_block.c`). The
regions keep their order, each starts where the one before ends, and with no request the result is the fixed layout
above.

Three things follow from a layout that moves:

- **The objective list's table.** Its 60 displacements from the block's start move with the list. The one negated
  displacement (0xF70F70, minus the list's start plus 4) moves the other way. The 13 displacements from the list's own
  start stay.
- **A self-check before any write.** `GenerationLayoutFailed` refuses the level when two regions overlap, when the
  block's size is not the last region's end, or when a value to be written addresses another region.
- **A guard on the mission stamps.** Their append (0xF8E935) takes no capacity. With a request, a stub at 0xF8E925
  leaves out a stamp past the last place and counts it.

Two more lists move only when a mod asks for more than the game's places: the important locations (G+0x8996B0, 256 x
0x108, 129 instructions) and the linked locations (G+0x8AB6C0, 64 x 0x18, 7 instructions). They go after the clusters,
and each keeps its place within 16 bytes. No instruction addresses a later element by a fixed offset, so the first
element's references carry the whole list. The terrain step's own reader follows the moved list. Important locations
stop at 1024, the size of the cities' flags.

The route graph moves the same way, each list by itself: nodes (G+0x8C4E40, 975 x 0x98), edges (G+0x937348, 1621 x
0x48) with their class bytes (G+0x11A1F98), and road points (G+0x9568E4, 9936 x 8). It has 182 instructions and 166
capacities. A capacity is a store into a pass record, beside the pointer to its list's count. GenerateRoutes clears
each list and sizes its hash tables by them. The limits are 2304 nodes, 3456 edges and 65520 points: a road entry names
a point in 16 bits. A hash over the block still covers the first 9936 points only.

A request makes the level need the moved block, even a level with no larger map or extra objectives. A level added
while the game runs keeps the arrays it found. Locations stop at 65024 because the stamp index keeps a location's id in
16 bits (0xF35054).

Blocks are made with a world, after the loader installs this, and if one exists already, the move is skipped. Structured
maps (map type 2, the mega cities, another generator) grow too. Their revoronoi, grid and vertex buckets are scaled the
same way (difficulty_cities.c).

### City streets

FlattenCityRoads (0xFAF130) gives a street its end pieces (+0x240/+0x250) and spaced props (+0x270) only up to their
reach past the playable radius, 30 m on most city types. The road-prop placer (0xFB1DB0) tests each position the same
way. On a larger map those four tests measure from the border, as the town placers do (difficulty_streets.c). So streets
are dressed wherever buildings stand. `HELLFORGE_D11_TOWN_BORDER=0` keeps the radius.

The road props per edge (`rand % type+0x230 + 1`, 0xFB2925) are a fixed count. On a larger map's 1.5 x longer edges the
count is x 3/2. `HELLFORGE_D11_STREET_PROPS=0` keeps it.

### Distance factors

The mission distance factors (10 levels x 3, read unclamped at 7 sites) move to a table with level 11's.

## Missions

The war table offers a level only the operations whose difficulty range includes it. Operation settings (25 rows) and
missions (162 rows) each have a lowest and highest level. Every range ending at 10 ends at 11. The game's random pick of
a difficulty within a mission's range keeps 10, so nothing picks 11 by chance.

## Mission board

The war table generates three missions per level into a 110-slot board per planet. The first 30 slots are the planet's
own (levels 1 to 10), then 10 for each of up to 8 regions. Level 11 takes region 7's ten: the planet's three and one for
each of regions 0 to 6.

A planet with a region 7 (only Cyberstan) offers no level 11 of its own, as it offers none at any level. It puts each
region's level 11 in that region's level 1 slot, which its regions (difficulty 4 and up) never use.
